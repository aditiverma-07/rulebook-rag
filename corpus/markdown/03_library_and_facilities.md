# Medicaps Institute of Technology — Academic & Student Regulations 2026

## 11. Library Regulations
### 11.1 Access, Membership, and Timings
The Central Library serves as the primary intellectual resource center for all students, faculty, researchers, and administrative staff at the Institute. It houses an extensive collection of print volumes, digital archives, journals, and multimedia resources. Access to the physical library premises is strictly restricted to registered members who must carry and scan their valid, physical university ID card at the turnstiles. Digital IDs are not accepted for entry. 

Library membership is automatically activated upon official enrollment and the payment of the initial library security deposit. The library maintains extensive operating hours to support student learning. It remains open from 8:00 AM to midnight continuously on regular weekdays. On weekends and public holidays, the hours are adjusted to 9:00 AM to 8:00 PM. Recognizing the intensive study requirements during assessment periods, the main reading rooms and reference sections remain open 24/7 during the official mid-semester and end-semester examination weeks. However, the circulation desk closes at 10:00 PM even during these 24/7 operational periods.

### 11.2 Borrowing Privileges and Responsibilities
Different membership categories carry varying borrowing privileges. Undergraduate students are permitted to borrow up to 5 books simultaneously for a maximum period of 14 days. Postgraduate students and doctoral researchers may borrow up to 8 books for an extended period of 21 days. Faculty members have custom limits determined by their department. 

Certain categories of materials are strictly non-circulating. Reference books, current issues of periodicals, rare historical collections, and thesis manuscripts are for in-library use only and cannot be checked out under any circumstances. All borrowed books must be returned in good condition on or before the stamped due date. A late fine of Rs. 10 per day is automatically levied by the library management system for overdue items. This fine doubles to Rs. 20 per day during examination weeks to encourage rapid circulation of high-demand textbooks. 

If a borrowed book is lost, severely damaged, or defaced with highlights and notes, the borrower is entirely responsible for restitution. The borrower must replace the item with a brand new, unused copy of the exact same edition or a newer edition. If the book is out of print or unavailable, the borrower must pay a penalty equal to three times the current, verified market price of the book to cover the cost of sourcing a replacement and administrative overhead. 

### 11.3 Digital Resources and Fair Use
The library provides access to dozens of premium online databases, academic journals, and digital archives. These resources are licensed exclusively for the personal, educational, and non-commercial use of the Institute's community. Systematic downloading, distributing licensed content to non-members, or using automated scraping scripts on library databases violates vendor licenses and the Institute's acceptable use policy. Such actions will result in the immediate revocation of all library privileges and referral to the Disciplinary Committee.

## 12. Academic Committees
### 12.1 Program Assessment Committee (PAC)
The Program Assessment Committee (PAC) is a vital quality assurance body responsible for monitoring the academic health and progress of specific degree programs offered by each department. The PAC systematically reviews course curricula, ensures alignment with national accreditation standards, evaluates aggregated student feedback from end-of-semester surveys, and suggests pedagogical improvements to enhance the quality of education. 

The PAC meets at least twice per academic semester. It is chaired by the Head of the Department and includes senior faculty, alumni representatives, and industry experts to ensure the curriculum remains relevant to current technological trends. The PAC submits its formal recommendations and audit reports to the Board of Studies (BoS) for approval and subsequent implementation.

### 12.2 Departmental Academic Committee (DAC)
While the PAC focuses on long-term programmatic goals, the Departmental Academic Committee (DAC) handles the immediate, day-to-day academic administration within a department. The DAC manages matters related to complex course registrations, the approval of new elective offerings, faculty teaching assignments, and the resolution of timetable conflicts. 

Crucially, the DAC also serves as the first line of intervention for students facing academic probation. It reviews the cases of students who have failed multiple courses or whose CGPA has dropped below the minimum threshold. The DAC conducts interviews with these students, analyzes their academic difficulties, and recommends targeted remedial actions, such as mandatory tutoring, reduced course loads, or a change of academic advisor, before recommending formal dismissal from the program.

### 12.3 The Academic Senate
The Academic Senate is the highest academic authority within the Institute. Chaired by the Director, it comprises all department heads, deans, and elected faculty representatives. The Senate approves all new degree programs, major curriculum overhauls, changes to grading policies, and the conferral of degrees during convocation. The decisions of the Academic Senate are supreme regarding the academic direction of the Institute.

## 13. Campus IT and Network Acceptable Use Policy
### 13.1 Network Access and Security
The Institute provides high-speed campus-wide Wi-Fi and wired network access intended purely for academic and research purposes. Every student is provided with a unique institutional email ID and network credentials. Sharing network credentials is a severe violation of the IT policy. Students are personally responsible for any digital activity originating from their authenticated sessions. Connecting personal wireless routers, unauthorized network switches, or attempting to bypass the institutional firewall and web-filters will lead to the permanent blocking of the device's MAC address and referral to the Disciplinary Committee. 

### 13.2 Software Licensing and Piracy
The Institute provides licenses for various engineering and statistical software packages via centralized license servers. These tools must only be used for university coursework and authorized research. Using university licenses for commercial freelance work is strictly prohibited. Downloading, distributing, or hosting pirated software, copyrighted movies, or illegal content using the university network is a serious offense that can expose the Institute to legal liability and will result in immediate suspension.

## 14. Campus Facilities and Support Services
### 14.1 Sports Complex and Gymnasium
The sprawling central Sports Complex includes synthetic tennis courts, an Olympic-size swimming pool, a fully equipped gymnasium, and tracks for athletics. Access is restricted to students with a valid Sports Pass, which is obtained at the beginning of the academic year by submitting a basic fitness declaration. The gymnasium operates from 5:30 AM to 9:30 AM and 4:30 PM to 9:30 PM. Professional trainers are available during evening hours. Proper athletic attire and non-marking shoes are strictly mandatory in indoor courts. Any damage to sports equipment due to negligence will be billed to the user.

### 14.2 Student Counseling Center
Recognizing the intense pressure of academic programs, the Institute funds a comprehensive Student Counseling Center. The center employs certified clinical psychologists and career counselors. Services are completely free and entirely confidential. Nothing discussed in therapy sessions is shared with the academic departments or parents unless there is an imminent, severe risk to life. Students can schedule walk-in sessions or book ongoing therapy programs to deal with homesickness, academic anxiety, depression, or career-related stress.

### 14.3 Transportation and Parking 
The Institute operates a fleet of bio-diesel buses on six major routes connecting the campus to the city center and major transit hubs. Bus passes are issued semester-wise. The campus strongly promotes eco-friendly transit and discourages unnecessary vehicular traffic. A comprehensive network of cycling lanes has been developed across the academic and residential zones to ensure a green, pollution-free environment. Commuting to and from classes via bicycles is the preferred mode of internal transit.
