# Medicaps Institute of Technology — Academic & Student Regulations 2026

## 1. Introduction and Scope
The Medicaps Institute of Technology (hereinafter referred to as the "Institute") is dedicated to fostering a rigorous, inclusive, and forward-thinking academic environment. These regulations govern all undergraduate and postgraduate academic programs offered by the Institute. It is the fundamental responsibility of every enrolled student to familiarize themselves with these policies. Ignorance of the regulations is not a valid excuse for non-compliance under any circumstances. These regulations take effect from the commencement of the Autumn Semester of the 2026 academic year, superseding all prior academic rules. The Institute reserves the right to amend, suspend, or revoke any regulation contained herein at its sole discretion, provided that students are given reasonable notice of such changes. In cases of ambiguity or dispute regarding academic matters, the interpretation provided by the Dean of Academic Affairs shall be final and binding. The Academic Senate oversees the implementation of these regulations and periodically reviews their effectiveness in maintaining the highest educational standards. Furthermore, these regulations apply equally to full-time, part-time, and exchange students unless explicitly exempted by a specific memorandum of understanding. The academic calendar, published annually by the Registrar's office, supplements these regulations and specifies all critical dates and deadlines. Any deviation from these rules requires formal approval through a written petition to the Academic Grievance Board.

## 2. Course Registration and Add/Drop Rules
### 2.1 Standard Course Registration
All students must complete their course registration online via the university ERP portal prior to the start of each semester. The registration window typically opens exactly four weeks before the first day of classes and closes rigidly on the first Friday of the semester at 5:00 PM local time. Students are strongly advised to consult with their assigned academic advisors before finalizing their schedules to ensure they meet all degree progression requirements. Registration is considered legally complete only when all outstanding tuition fees, hostel dues, library fines, and any other encumbrances from previous semesters have been fully cleared. The system will automatically block registration for students with unpaid dues exceeding Rs. 500. 

Late registration is permitted only under highly exceptional circumstances, such as a documented medical emergency involving hospitalization, severe family bereavement, or a demonstrable administrative error on the part of the Institute. Any late registration requires the explicit written approval of the Head of the Department and the Dean of Academic Affairs. A non-refundable late registration fee will be applied to all such cases, escalating daily. Students registering late are entirely responsible for making up any missed coursework, quizzes, or assignments. Faculty are not obligated to offer remedial sessions for late registrants.

### 2.2 Add/Drop Period
The Institute provides a grace period to adjust academic schedules. Students may add or drop elective or open courses without any academic penalty during the official Add/Drop period, which spans strictly the first two weeks of the semester. Courses dropped during this period will not appear on the student's official transcript. Adding a course during this period is subject to physical classroom seating capacity, timetable clashes, and the satisfaction of all prerequisite conditions. Overriding prerequisites is generally discouraged and requires the signature of the course instructor and the department chair. 

If a student drops a course after the Add/Drop period but before the official withdrawal deadline (which falls precisely at the end of the eighth week of classes), a grade of 'W' (Withdrawal) will be permanently recorded on the transcript. This 'W' grade does not affect the calculation of the Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) or the Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA), but it remains as a record of attempted coursework. Withdrawals after the eighth week are categorically forbidden unless approved by the Academic Appeals Committee under severe, unforeseen extenuating circumstances such as a prolonged incapacitating illness. Unapproved abandonment of a course after the eighth week will result in an automatic 'F' grade.

### 2.3 Maximum and Minimum Credit Load
To maintain academic rigor and ensure timely graduation, students must adhere to credit limits. The normal course load for an undergraduate semester is between 18 and 22 credits. Students on academic probation are restricted to a maximum of 16 credits. Exceptionally high-performing students with a CGPA above 8.5 may petition their department to take an overload up to 26 credits. The minimum credit load to maintain full-time student status is 12 credits. Falling below 12 credits without prior authorization from the Dean's office may jeopardize academic standing, scholarships, and student visa status.

## 3. Attendance Requirements
### 3.1 Minimum Attendance Policy
The Institute mandates strict, continuous attendance for all scheduled academic activities. This includes lectures, tutorials, laboratory sessions, design studios, and mandatory seminars. A student must maintain a minimum attendance of 75% in each individual course to be eligible to appear for the end-semester examination in that course. Attendance is calculated strictly from the first day of the semester as per the academic calendar, regardless of the student's actual date of registration or add/drop activity. 

The course instructor is responsible for maintaining accurate, daily attendance records and publishing them on the student ERP portal on a fortnightly basis. Students are responsible for monitoring their own attendance percentage. Any discrepancies in attendance records must be brought to the instructor's attention within five working days of the publication of the fortnightly report. Retroactive corrections to attendance records are not permitted. 

Students whose attendance falls below the 75% threshold without approved condonation will be automatically awarded an 'F-A' (Fail due to Attendance) grade for the course. An 'F-A' grade prevents the student from writing the final examination and requires them to repeat the entire course when it is next offered. There are no supplementary examinations for students who receive an 'F-A' grade.

### 3.2 Attendance Shortage and Condonation
The Institute recognizes that under extraordinary, unavoidable circumstances, an exception to the strict minimum attendance rule may be necessary. Such circumstances are strictly limited to severe personal illness, hospitalization, or the death of an immediate family member. 

The Principal may condone an attendance shortage of up to 10% for valid medical reasons. This means that with condonation, the absolute minimum attendance permitted under this section is 65%. To apply for this condonation, the student must submit a formal, written petition along with verifiable documentation (such as a medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner) to the Head of the Department before the last instructional day of the semester. 

Condonation is not an automatic right but a privilege granted entirely at the discretion of the Principal upon recommendation by the Head of the Department. The committee reviewing condonation applications will consider the student's overall academic performance, past attendance record, and the severity of the stated reason. Applications submitted after the commencement of the end-semester examinations will be summarily rejected.

### 3.3 Duty Leave
Students participating in approved extracurricular activities, university-sponsored sports tournaments, or academic conferences representing the Institute may be granted "Duty Leave" (DL). Duty Leave is considered equivalent to attending the class for the purpose of calculating the attendance percentage. However, the total duration of Duty Leave cannot exceed 10% of the total scheduled classes for any course. Prior approval from the Dean of Student Affairs and the respective course instructors is mandatory before availing Duty Leave.

## 4. Medical Leave and Exemptions
### 4.1 Applying for Medical Leave
Students experiencing significant health issues that prevent them from attending classes, tutorials, or laboratories for more than three consecutive instructional days must officially apply for medical leave. The application must be submitted directly to the University Health Center. It must include a detailed, original certificate from a registered medical practitioner or hospital. The certificate must clearly state the exact nature of the illness, the recommended period of complete rest, and the date the student is officially declared fit to resume regular academic activities. 

The University Health Center reserves the right to verify the authenticity of the medical certificate with the issuing physician or hospital. Fraudulent medical certificates will be forwarded to the Disciplinary Committee and may result in immediate expulsion from the Institute.

### 4.2 Impact on Continuous Assessment
If a student misses a mid-term examination or a scheduled continuous assessment due to approved medical leave, the instructor has the discretion to either administer a make-up examination or proportionally scale the weightage of the end-semester examination to cover the missed assessment. The format and timing of any make-up assessment are determined solely by the course instructor. Make-up assessments must be completed within two weeks of the student's return to campus. 

### 4.5 Medical Leave Documentation Deadline
Timely submission of medical records is critical for the approval of medical leave and any associated attendance condonation or assessment exemptions. Students must submit valid medical documentation within 7 calendar days of returning to campus. Failure to submit the documentation within this strict 7-day window will result in the automatic rejection of the medical leave application, and the student will be marked absent for the entire duration of the illness, with no possibility of make-up assessments. Exceptions to this deadline are extremely rare and are only made in cases where the student was physically incapacitated and entirely unable to communicate, as verified by a hospital admissions record.

## 5. Examinations and Internal Assessment
### 5.1 Structure of Assessment
The evaluation of a student's performance in a theoretical course is continuously assessed throughout the semester. The assessment is divided into Internal Assessment (IA) and the End-Semester Examination (ESE). The standard weightage assigned is 40% for IA and 60% for ESE, although this may vary for laboratory, studio, and project-based courses as specified in the course syllabus. 

The Internal Assessment comprises various components including surprise quizzes, scheduled mid-term examinations, take-home assignments, class participation, and group presentations. The exact breakdown of the IA is announced by the course instructor during the first week of classes. To pass a course, a student must secure a minimum combined score in the IA and ESE, typically set at 35% or 40% depending on the program regulations.

### 5.2 End-Semester Examinations
End-Semester Examinations are rigorous evaluations conducted by the central Examination Cell at the conclusion of the academic term. The timetable is published at least four weeks in advance. Students must carry their physical, valid university ID card and the official printed hall ticket to the examination venue. Digital copies are not accepted.

Strict punctuality is enforced. Arriving more than 30 minutes after the official commencement of the examination will result in denied entry to the hall, and the student will be marked absent. No student is allowed to leave the examination hall during the first hour or the last fifteen minutes of the examination, regardless of whether they have finished writing. Restroom breaks are permitted only after the first hour, strictly under the supervision of an invigilator. 

## 6. Grading and Revaluation
### 6.1 Grading System
The Institute follows a comprehensive 10-point absolute grading system. The letter grades and their corresponding grade points are standard across all departments: 
- O (Outstanding): 10 grade points. Given for exceptional performance and original thinking.
- A+ (Excellent): 9 grade points.
- A (Very Good): 8 grade points.
- B+ (Good): 7 grade points.
- B (Above Average): 6 grade points.
- C (Average): 5 grade points.
- P (Pass): 4 grade points. The minimum passing grade for undergraduate courses.
- F (Fail): 0 grade points. Indicates performance below the required standard. 

The CGPA is calculated as the weighted average of the grade points earned in all courses completed, weighted by the credit value of each course. A minimum CGPA of 5.0 is required for graduation.

### 6.2 Revaluation Process
If a student firmly believes there has been an error in the grading of their End-Semester Examination answer script, they may apply for official revaluation. This application must be submitted to the Examination Cell within two weeks (14 days) of the formal declaration of results on the university portal. 

The revaluation process involves a blind re-grading of the entire answer script by a different, independent faculty member appointed by the Controller of Examinations. If the new grade differs from the original grade by more than one full letter grade (e.g., from a B to an A), the higher of the two grades will be officially awarded, and the revaluation fee will be completely refunded to the student's university account. If the change is less than one full letter grade, the original grade stands, and the fee is forfeit. The result of the revaluation process is final and cannot be appealed further. Revaluation applies only to the End-Semester Examination and not to Internal Assessment components, practical exams, or project vivas.
