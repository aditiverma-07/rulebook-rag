# Medicaps Institute of Technology — Academic & Student Regulations 2026

## 7. Student Discipline and Code of Conduct
### 7.1 General Principles of Conduct
The Medicaps Institute of Technology expects all students to maintain the absolute highest standards of personal ethics, academic integrity, and civic responsibility. The Code of Conduct applies universally to student behavior on the main campus, in all university-affiliated housing and hostels, during official university events off-campus, on university-provided transportation, and during official industrial visits or study tours. 

Enrollment at the Institute represents a voluntary agreement by the student to adhere to these standards. The Institute views discipline not merely as a punitive system, but as an essential educational framework necessary for the holistic development of professionals. Violations of the Code of Conduct disrupt the academic environment and harm the university community. Such violations include, but are not limited to, physical assault, harassment (including sexual and digital harassment), bullying, intentional vandalism of university property, substance abuse (including the possession or consumption of alcohol and illegal narcotics), theft, and insubordination towards university staff and faculty.

### 7.2 The Disciplinary Committee
The Disciplinary Committee (DC) is the primary body empowered to investigate alleged violations of the Code of Conduct and impose appropriate sanctions. The committee is chaired by the Dean of Student Affairs and includes senior faculty members, a legal advisor, and a student representative (who recuses themselves in cases involving academic confidentiality). 

Upon receiving a formal complaint, the DC will issue a show-cause notice to the accused student. The student is granted a fair hearing to present their defense, produce evidence, and call witnesses. The DC operates on the principle of the balance of probabilities. Sanctions range from official written warnings, mandatory community service on campus, and monetary fines to temporary suspension for one or more semesters, and in severe cases, permanent expulsion from the Institute. The Institute also maintains a strict zero-tolerance policy towards ragging and any form of discrimination based on race, gender, religion, caste, or nationality. Ragging in any form is a criminal offense and will lead to immediate expulsion and the filing of a First Information Report (FIR) with the local police.

## 8. Examination Malpractice
### 8.1 Definition and Scope of Malpractice
Examination malpractice is viewed as a fundamental breach of academic integrity and includes any attempt, successful or otherwise, to gain an unfair advantage during an assessment. This encompasses a wide range of prohibited activities. Bringing unauthorized materials, such as handwritten notes, textbooks, cheat sheets, or programmable calculators (unless explicitly specified by the examiner), into the examination hall is strictly forbidden. 

Furthermore, copying from another student's answer script, verbally communicating with others during the exam, impersonating another student, and allowing oneself to be impersonated are grave offenses. In the context of internal assessments, plagiarism—submitting someone else's work, ideas, or text without proper citation—in assignments, essays, or project reports constitutes severe malpractice. The possession or use of any unauthorized electronic devices is strictly prohibited during examinations and constitutes immediate malpractice, regardless of whether the device was actually used.

### 8.2 Procedure for Handling Malpractice and Absences
When an invigilator or faculty member suspects malpractice during a live examination, they are instructed to intervene immediately. The invigilator will confiscate the student's answer script, the unauthorized materials (if any), and any electronic devices involved. The student will be provided with a new, fresh answer script to complete the remaining duration of the examination, ensuring they are not deprived of the allotted time while the investigation is pending. 

A detailed incident report, co-signed by the Chief Superintendent of Examinations, will be forwarded to the Malpractice Inquiry Committee within 24 hours. The Committee will then convene to conduct a formal hearing where the student is given an opportunity to present their side of the story. If the student is found guilty, sanctions can include the cancellation of the examination result for that specific course, the cancellation of results for all courses in that semester, suspension for an entire academic year, or permanent expulsion, depending entirely on the premeditation and severity of the offense. 

Furthermore, procedural compliance is required for all committee hearings. If a student misses a scheduled disciplinary hearing or an examination due to sudden health reasons, verifiable documentation is strictly required. Any medical certificate supporting an absence must be filed with the disciplinary committee within 5 working days of the missed event. Failure to do so will result in an ex-parte decision by the committee based on the available evidence, and the student forfeits their right to appeal the procedural timeline.

## 9. Academic Appeals and Grievances
### 9.1 Right to Appeal Decisions
The Institute ensures that all students have access to a fair, transparent, and impartial appeals process. Students have the explicit right to appeal decisions related to their academic standing (such as academic probation or dismissal), disciplinary actions imposed by the DC, and findings of examination malpractice. 

The appeal must be submitted in writing to the Chairman of the Academic Appeals Board. This submission must occur strictly within 14 calendar days of the student receiving the official, written decision from the relevant committee. Appeals submitted after the 14-day window will not be entertained under any circumstances. The written appeal must clearly state the specific grounds for the appeal. The permitted grounds are strictly limited to: (a) demonstrable procedural errors that significantly prejudiced the outcome of the original hearing, (b) the discovery of compelling new evidence that was not previously available to the student during the original investigation, or (c) a claim that the penalty imposed is vastly disproportionate to the nature of the offense. Disagreement with the severity of a standard penalty is not a valid ground for appeal.

### 9.2 The Appeals Board and Finality
The Academic Appeals Board is a high-level body composed of the Director of the Institute, three senior professors who were not involved in the original decision, and the Registrar. The Board reviews the documentary evidence and may choose to call the student for an in-person interview. The Board's decision is final, conclusive, and binding on all parties within the university hierarchy. Once the Appeals Board issues its ruling, no further administrative recourse is available within the Institute.

### 9.3 Grievance Redressal Mechanism
For issues outside the scope of formal disciplinary or academic appeals, the Institute provides a General Grievance Redressal Cell. This cell handles complaints regarding inadequate campus facilities, discrimination by peers or staff, administrative delays, and issues related to scholarships. Students can file a grievance via the anonymous online portal or in person at the Dean of Student Affairs office. The Grievance Cell is legally mandated to acknowledge receipt of the complaint within 48 hours and provide a preliminary investigative report within 15 working days. The identity of the complainant is kept strictly confidential during the initial investigation phase.

## 10. Hostel Regulations and Residency
### 10.1 Residency Requirements and Curfew
To foster a cohesive campus community, all first-year undergraduate students are required to reside in the university hostels, subject to room availability. For senior students, hostel accommodation is highly competitive and is allocated on a first-come, first-served basis, heavily prioritizing students from out of state and those with excellent academic records. 

Residents must adhere strictly to the hostel curfew times for their own safety and the security of the campus. The main entry gates to all hostel blocks are securely locked at 10:30 PM on weekdays (Sunday to Thursday) and at 11:30 PM on weekends (Friday and Saturday). Late entry is permitted only with prior, verifiable written permission from the Chief Hostel Warden, usually granted only for late-running laboratory work or university-sanctioned events. 

### 10.2 Room Allocation, Upkeep, and Prohibited Items
Students are completely responsible for the furniture, electrical fixtures, and overall upkeep of their assigned rooms. A detailed inventory is signed upon move-in. Any damage discovered upon move-out will be billed directly to the occupants, often resulting in deductions from the security deposit. 

The use of high-wattage electrical appliances is heavily regulated. Devices such as room heaters, electric hot plates, induction cookers, immersion rods, and high-capacity kettles are strictly forbidden due to severe fire safety regulations and limitations on the hostel electrical grid. Discovery of these items will lead to immediate confiscation and a heavy fine. Furthermore, subletting the assigned room, swapping rooms without administrative approval, or allowing unauthorized guests (including family members) to stay overnight is considered a severe security breach. Such offenses will lead to immediate, summary eviction from the hostel without refund, and potential further disciplinary action.

### 10.3 Mess Regulations and Dining
The hostel mess is managed by a centralized contractor overseen by the Student Mess Council. Attendance at the mess is mandatory for all hostel residents; no exemptions are granted for eating outside. The mess operates strictly during designated meal hours. Wasting food is treated as a severe ethical violation, and repeated instances may lead to fines imposed by the Mess Warden. Taking utensils, plates, or prepared food out of the dining hall into the individual hostel rooms is strictly prohibited to maintain hygiene and prevent pest infestations. Special dietary requirements for medical reasons must be communicated to the Chief Dietician at the beginning of the semester with accompanying medical certification.
